Pretrain to Adapt: What Makes a
Pretrained Policy Adaptable?

"The most capable pretrained model is not necessarily
the easiest to adapt."

November 12, 2026 Austin, TX CoRL 2026

About

Overview

The strongest robotic manipulation systems today are typically built in two stages: first, pretrain a generalist policy on a large and diverse dataset, then adapt it to the specific setting where we actually want to deploy it. Adaptation can take many forms — supervised and RL finetuning, in-context learning and test-time steering, and targeting not just specific tasks but also new embodiments and environments — and is often critical for achieving effective performance in the deployment domain of interest.

These two stages optimize for different objectives, however: pretraining targets broad competence across many tasks, while adaptation targets the specific deployment setting of interest. The most capable pretrained model is not necessarily the easiest to adapt, and we have no reliable way to tell, before adaptation, whether one pretrained policy is a better starting point for adaptation than another.

This workshop seeks to bridge these two stages and ask what makes a pretrained policy a good foundation for adaptation? Our goal is to bring together researchers working on pretraining generalist policies with those developing approaches for adaptation, to discuss how pretraining and adaptation should be designed together.

Focus

Core Research Questions

🎯

Pretraining Objectives

  • Behavior cloning vs. return-conditioned, world-model, and value-based objectives
  • Few-shot and in-context pretraining objectives
  • Steerability vs. plasticity, and how to improve both
🗄️

Pretraining Data for Adaptability

  • Demonstrations, play data, failures, and RL rollouts
  • What data properties drive zero-shot vs. adaptable performance
  • Data diversity across objects, scenes, skills, and embodiments
🔗

Coupling Pretraining & Adaptation

  • RL finetuning, in-context learning, and test-time steering
  • How pretraining recipes should depend on the adaptation method
  • Adapting to new tasks, embodiments, and environments
📊

Measuring Readiness to Adapt

  • Beyond train/validation loss as a predictor of adaptability
  • How cheaply a model can be steered toward new behaviors
  • Offline metrics that predict downstream adaptation success

Submissions

Call for Papers

Topics

We welcome submissions on, but not limited to:

  • Pretraining objectives for adaptable policies (behavior cloning, return-conditioned, world-model, value-based, in-context objectives)
  • Composing and curating heterogeneous pretraining data (demonstrations, play data, suboptimal/failure trajectories, RL rollouts) for downstream adaptability
  • Coupling pretraining recipes with adaptation methods: RL finetuning, in-context learning, and test-time steering
  • Steerability and plasticity of pretrained policies
  • Adapting pretrained policies to new tasks, embodiments, and environments
  • Metrics and benchmarks for measuring readiness to adapt
  • World models as pretraining objectives, evaluation tools, or RL environments
  • Reward modeling and learned rewards for continual policy improvement
  • Position papers, work-in-progress, and retrospectives on any of the above

Submissions and reviews will be managed through OpenReview (link to open with the call). We expect 20–30 submissions; accepted papers will be presented in an interactive poster session.
